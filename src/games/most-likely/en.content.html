How to play Most Likely To

Most Likely To is one of the simplest and funniest group games to play at home: no cards, no board and no preparation needed. A sentence such as “Who's most likely to get lost even with GPS on?” appears on screen and everyone points at the person in the group they think fits it best. It's the perfect icebreaker for a birthday party, a sleepover or a relaxed game night with friends.

Step-by-step rules

  1. Sit in a circle so everyone can see each other. One player acts as host and holds the phone or laptop, or casts the game to the TV.
  2. Choose a question pack: Friends, Family or Colleagues.
  3. The host reads the sentence out loud and presses “Count down and vote”: a three-second countdown starts.
  4. When it ends, everyone points at their pick at the same time. No changing your mind!
  5. Whoever gets the most votes takes on the forfeit shown on screen. If there's a tie, all the top-voted players do it.
  6. Move on to the next question. When you want to stop, press “End game”.

How many players do you need?

The game works with 3 or more players, but it shines with a group of 5 to 12: the more of you there are, the more the votes split and the more surprising the results. A game lasts around 15-20 minutes on average, but you can stop whenever you like.

Question packs

  • Friends: silly everyday situations, great for a night in or a party.
  • Family: questions for all ages, to play with parents, grandparents and kids.
  • Colleagues: office-themed prompts, perfect for team building or a work dinner.

Questions are shuffled every game and never repeat until the whole deck has been played.

Fun forfeits for everyone

Instead of the usual party dares, PartyGames suggests light, harmless challenges: imitate an animal, speak in rhyme, tell a joke or invent a dance move. Don't like a forfeit? Press “Another forfeit”. You can also skip forfeits entirely and just keep track of who gets the most votes.

Tips for the host

  • Read the sentence dramatically and build a little suspense before the countdown.
  • After the vote, ask someone to explain their choice: that's where the best laughs come from.
  • Remind everyone it's just a game: the prompts are playful and should never embarrass anyone.
  • Cast the game to your TV: the large text is easy to read from across the room.

Frequently asked questions

Can you vote for yourself?

Yes, if the group agrees. Many groups ban it to keep the vote honest: decide before you start.

What happens if there's a tie?

All the top-voted players do the forfeit together, or you can hold a quick re-vote between them with a new countdown.

Is the game suitable for kids?

Yes: the Family pack is written for all ages and the forfeits are always harmless.

Do I need an Internet connection while playing?

You only need to load the page once: the questions are already in your browser and the game keeps working even on a slow connection.
