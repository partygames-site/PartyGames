How to play Moral Dilemmas

Moral Dilemmas is the game of impossible choices, also known as Would You Rather. A question with two options, A and B, appears on screen: there's no right answer, what matters is defending your choice. It's a perfect conversation game for couples, groups of friends, family dinners and any evening when you want to get to know each other better.

Step-by-step rules

  1. Pick a type of dilemma: Moral dilemmas for deeper debates, or For a million… for light, silly challenges.
  2. The host reads the question and both options out loud.
  3. Everyone thinks about their choice, then on “go” raises a hand for A or B. Tap the options on screen to count the votes.
  4. Press “Debate”: a 30-second timer starts while each side explains its reasons.
  5. When time runs out, anyone can switch sides. Whoever wins over the most people takes the round.
  6. Move on to the next dilemma, or press “End game” whenever you want to stop.

How many players do you need?

Just 2 people are enough: as a couple, the game is a fun way to discover how the other person thinks. In a group of 4 to 10 players the debates get livelier because real opinion teams form. A session lasts around 20 minutes on average.

Two game modes

  • Moral dilemmas: truth or white lie, career or family, past or future. Thought-provoking questions that never get too heavy.
  • For a million…: would you give up pizza forever? Go to work in pyjamas for a year? Absurd challenges guaranteed to get everyone laughing.

Tips for a fun debate

  • Always ask why someone picked an option: that's where the game gets interesting.
  • Respect everyone's opinion: the goal is to compare views, not to argue.
  • Give a point to anyone who makes someone change their mind: the most persuasive player wins.
  • On couples' nights, try to guess what your partner will choose before they answer.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between Moral Dilemmas and Would You Rather?

They're the same kind of game: a choice between two options. Moral Dilemmas adds a debate timer and a vote counter, which makes it easy to play in a group.

Are the questions suitable for everyone?

Yes. The dilemmas are written for a general audience: they make you think or laugh, but avoid violent or adult topics.

Can we play remotely?

Yes: share your screen on a video call and vote by typing A or B in the chat.

How many dilemmas are there?

More than 80 across the two modes. They're shuffled every game and never repeat until the whole deck has been played.
