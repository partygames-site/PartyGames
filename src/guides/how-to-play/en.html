Every game on PartyGames Club works the same way: one person runs the game from a laptop, tablet or phone, the screen is shown to everyone, and players compete individually or in teams. Here is everything you need to know before your first round.

What you need

  • One device with a web browser: a laptop is ideal, but a tablet or phone works too. Nothing to install and no account.
  • A big screen, if you can: a TV or a projector, so everyone can read the questions at the same time. For two to four players, a laptop screen is enough.
  • Pen and paper for a few games where teams write down their answers.
  • A game master: the person in charge of the game. More on this role below.

How to show the game on a TV or projector

Party games are at their best on a big screen, with everyone facing it like a quiz show. Pick whichever option you have at home:

  • HDMI cable: the simplest and most reliable. Connect the laptop to the TV or projector and select the right input.
  • Casting: with Chromecast or Google TV, open the game in Chrome and choose "Cast" from the browser menu. On Apple devices, use AirPlay screen mirroring to an Apple TV or a compatible TV.
  • The TV's own browser: many smart TVs have a web browser. Open the site directly on the TV and use the remote, although a laptop is more comfortable for the game master.

Tip: put the browser in full screen (F11 on most computers) so the game fills the whole TV, and set the device not to go to sleep during the evening.

The game master: the most important role

Every game needs a game master, the person who holds the device and runs the evening. The game master:

  1. Explains the rules before each game, ideally with a practice round with no points.
  2. Forms the teams, keeping them balanced and mixing people who know each other less.
  3. Reads the questions, presses the buttons and reveals the answers.
  4. Awards the points on the scoreboard.
  5. Settles disputes. When two teams both claim the point, or an answer is "almost right", the game master decides. That is the basic rule, and accepting it before you start avoids endless arguments.

Can the game master play too?

It depends on the game. As a general rule:

  • Yes in games where the challenge appears for everyone at the same moment and the master only presses buttons, such as reflex and observation games like Skill Test or quick-fire rounds like Fire Down.
  • Better not in games where the master has to check answers against a list or sees the solution before the others, such as Guess the Top 10. In these games the master acts as a neutral judge.

A good compromise at long game nights is to rotate the role: a different person becomes game master for each game, so everyone gets to play.

At my game nights I am almost always the game master: I connect the PC to the TV, choose the teams and the games and assign the points. The secret is managing the room: keeping the pace up, involving those who speak less and, above all, having the final word when two teams both think they have won the point.

Teams and points

Most games are played in teams. On the home page you will find the "Your teams" panel, where you choose how many teams there are, their names and colours. These settings are shared by every team game on the site, so:

  • you set up the teams once at the start of the evening;
  • points add up from one game to the next, creating an overall leaderboard for the evening;
  • everything is saved on your device only, so you can pick up the next evening where you left off, or reset the scores with one click.

Teams of two to four people work best. With many players, add a third or fourth team rather than making teams bigger.

Quick start in five steps

  1. Open the site on the game master's device and connect it to the TV.
  2. On the home page, set the number of teams and their names.
  3. Choose a game: each card shows the number of players and the average length.
  4. Read the "Rules and tips" section under the game area, then explain the rules to everyone.
  5. Play a practice round, then start for real.

Frequently asked questions

Does every player need a phone?

No. Only the game master needs a device. Everyone else just watches the shared screen and answers out loud or on paper.

Can the game master also play?

Yes in games where the challenge appears for everyone at the same time, like reflex games; it is better not to in games where the master checks answers or sees the solution first. Rotating the role is a good compromise.

How do I put the game on the TV?

With an HDMI cable from the laptop, by casting the browser tab with Chromecast or Google TV, with AirPlay on Apple devices, or by opening the site in the smart TV's own browser.

Are scores saved between games?

Yes. Teams and scores are shared by all team games and saved on your device, so the leaderboard carries over from one game to the next.
