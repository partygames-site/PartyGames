New Year's Eve at home with friends is a very long night: people arrive around eight, you have dinner, and there are still hours to go until midnight. The right games turn that wait into the best part of the night. Here is which games to choose, the order to play them in and a ready-made plan that takes you all the way to the countdown.

Why a New Year's Eve party needs a plan

A normal dinner party ends whenever it ends. New Year's Eve doesn't: there is a fixed time, midnight, and four or five hours to fill. Without a plan, the same things always happen: half the group is on their phones, someone falls asleep on the sofa, and at 11:50 everyone realises midnight is close only because the TV has started its countdown.

The trick is to treat the night like a show that builds up: relaxed, chatty games at the start, team challenges after dinner and a fast, loud finale that peaks right at midnight.

When guests arrive: icebreaker games

New Year's Eve often mixes different groups: old friends, new partners, colleagues, a friend of a friend. In the first half hour you need games with no points, which get everyone talking without putting anyone on the spot.

  • Most Likely To: a sentence like "who is most likely to be late for their own wedding?" appears on screen and everyone votes. It works even better if you turn some questions into a review of the year: "who changed their mind the most this year?".
  • Moral Dilemmas: impossible "would you rather…?" choices. There are no right answers, only debates. Perfect while the table is still being set.

At the table: games you can play without getting up

During dinner nobody wants to move to the TV, but between courses there is room for a light game, perhaps with the laptop at the end of the table.

  • Who Knows Who Best: ideal if there are couples or best friends at the table. You find out who really knows the other, and the wrong answers are often the funniest moments of the night.
  • Hidden Word Link: five clues and one word to find. You play out loud, even with your mouth full.

Tip: pick the teams at the table and keep them the same all night. The New Year's Eve championship becomes the thread running through the evening, and every game after that counts towards the final leaderboard.

After dinner: the team championship

This is the heart of the night. Connect your device to the TV (if you are not sure how, read the guide on how to play with a game master, a TV and teams), set up the teams on the home page and play two or three games in a row. On PartyGames Club the points add up from one game to the next, so at the end you have a real leaderboard.

  • Guess the Top 10: a hidden chart that teams fill in one answer at a time. It is the game that sparks the most arguments, perfect while everyone still has plenty of energy.
  • Emoji Song Quiz: famous song titles hidden in a row of emoji. Someone usually starts singing before they have even worked out the title.
  • Name Place Animal Speed: the fast version of the classic word game, team against team. People of every age enjoy it.

Close to midnight: the grand finale

In the last half hour the energy has to rise. Choose a game with very short rounds, where people shout out answers and anyone who hesitates is out:

  • Fire Down: a category and five seconds to answer. Hesitate or repeat a word and you are out; the last player standing wins.
  • Lightning Duel: a barrage of mini challenges in logic, memory and reflexes, each worth one point. Great for turning the leaderboard upside down at the last moment.

Stop around 11:50, announce the winning team and leave the final minutes for the countdown.

A game to pick up again next New Year's Eve

An idea that needs no screen at all: before midnight, everyone writes three predictions for the new year on a slip of paper, about themselves or the others. "Mark will change jobs", "we will finally go on holiday together", "someone will get married". The slips go into an envelope that is opened the following New Year's Eve: reading them a year later is always one of the funniest moments of the night.

Prizes and forfeits

A symbolic prize makes the championship feel much more important: choosing the music for the rest of the night, the right to skip clearing the table, or the official title of "champions of the year" until next New Year's Eve. Keep forfeits light: an impression, the chorus of a song, a thirty-second improvised New Year speech. The point is for everyone to laugh together, not to embarrass anyone.

Practical tips

  • Test everything before the guests arrive: the TV connection, the volume and a charged device.
  • Set the device not to go to sleep and put the browser in full screen.
  • Keep an eye on the clock: it is easy to get caught up in the game and notice midnight at the very last second.
  • With only a few guests, go for games for couples or two players, such as Exact Number and Who Knows Who Best.

Frequently asked questions

What games can you play at a New Year's Eve party at home?

Icebreakers work well when guests arrive, like Most Likely To, followed by a team championship after dinner with quizzes and charts, and a fast finale such as Fire Down just before the countdown.

How do you make New Year's Eve at home fun?

Give the night a build-up: relaxed games at the start, team challenges after dinner and a grand finale close to midnight. Keeping the same teams all night creates a leaderboard that keeps everyone involved until the end.

What games work on New Year's Eve with just a few people?

With two or four people, games for couples work best, such as Who Knows Who Best, Exact Number or Moral Dilemmas, which don't need big teams.

Do I need to download an app?

No. Every game runs in the browser of a phone, computer, tablet or smart TV, free and with no sign-up. All you need is one device connected to the TV.
