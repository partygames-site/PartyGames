The first fifteen minutes of a gathering where people do not know each other are the hardest. Conversations stay polite and short, and small groups form around the people who already know each other. A well-chosen icebreaker game changes that quickly. Here is how to pick one, the order to play them in and the mistakes to avoid.

From Francesco's experience

When I went on Erasmus to Sweden I arrived a week after everyone else, when they had all already met. I had to introduce myself to everyone without feeling awkward, and then I invited them all to dinner at my place so we could get to know each other as quickly as possible.

What makes a good icebreaker

An icebreaker has one job: to get people talking to each other, not just to the host. The best ones share four features:

  • No wrong answers. People who have just met are afraid of looking silly. Opinion and voting games remove that risk.
  • Low personal exposure at first. Start with light topics and only move to more personal ones once people are laughing.
  • Everyone takes part at the same time, so nobody is stuck in the spotlight while others watch.
  • They create stories. A funny answer becomes a running joke for the rest of the evening, and running jokes are what turn strangers into a group.

The right order: from light to lively

Do not start with a competitive game. Build up in three steps.

Step 1: opinions and votes (10 minutes)

Moral Dilemmas is ideal as the very first game. Everyone chooses between two impossible options and explains why. There is no right answer, it reveals personalities and it gets people disagreeing in a friendly way, which is exactly how conversations start.

Step 2: light teasing (10–15 minutes)

Once people have heard each other speak, move to Most Likely To. Everyone votes for who is most likely to do what is on the screen. With people who have just met, it is surprisingly fun: first impressions become the topic of conversation. Keep the forfeits light and optional.

Tip: in a group of strangers, ask people to say their name each time they vote or answer during the first rounds. By the end of the game, everyone will remember everyone else.

Step 3: mixed teams (20 minutes or more)

Now the group is ready for competition. Form teams that deliberately mix people who did not know each other, then play a quick team game like Fire Down or Odd One Out. Working together for points is the fastest way to build a sense of "us".

Icebreakers for specific situations

  • Couples meeting other couples: Who Knows Who Best lets each couple show off how well they know each other, and makes great conversation material.
  • A new class, course or club: keep it short and avoid anything personal. Opinion games and quick team quizzes are safest.
  • Friends of friends at a party: start with votes and dilemmas, then mix the friend groups into new teams.
  • Online groups: share your screen with the game, and ask everyone to keep their camera on during the first rounds.

From Francesco's experience

For getting to know strangers, my favourite is a classic "would you rather" game like Moral Dilemmas: it shows you how the other person thinks, lets you compare your different choices and quickly reveals who is stubborn and never changes their mind. From there, the party gets going.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Starting with personal questions. "Tell us your biggest fear" is too much for people who met five minutes ago.
  • Putting one person on the spot. Solo performances in front of strangers make shy people want to leave.
  • Letting friend groups stay together. If people who already know each other stay on the same team, the icebreaker has not done its job.
  • Going on too long. An icebreaker is a starter, not the main course. Stop when conversations start happening on their own.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best icebreaker game for people who don't know each other?

Start with an opinion game with no wrong answers, such as Moral Dilemmas, then move to voting games and finally to mixed team games.

How long should an icebreaker last?

Ten to twenty minutes. Its purpose is to start conversations, so stop as soon as people are chatting on their own.

How do I help shy guests feel included?

Choose games where everyone answers at the same time, avoid solo performances and put shy guests on teams with friendly, inclusive people.
